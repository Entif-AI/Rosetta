## The purpose of the legal design

Entif’s proposed public-benefit structure should preserve its mission and make consequential power accountable. The counsel-facing design brief asks how those outcomes can be translated into lawful organizing instruments, bylaws, policies and agreements.

This page presents the public legal-design objectives. The underlying counsel-facing brief remains an internal preparatory document; this overview does not reproduce confidential advice, negotiations, personal disclosures or protected source material.

## Outcomes the structure should preserve

- A public-benefit purpose that can survive changes in leadership, funding and technology.
- A mission lock with an identifiable actor, enforcement route, remedy and amendment process.
- Reserved powers and independent review for consequential and constitutional decisions.
- Board independence, conflict management and founder continuity without founder immunity.
- Protection of mission-bearing assets, lawful restrictions and third-party rights.
- Funding relationships that cannot silently buy authority over research findings or protected interests.

These are proposed design outcomes. Their legal availability and the instruments that can achieve them require qualified review.

## Questions for qualified counsel

The work includes comparing relevant jurisdictions, testing amendment and emergency mechanisms, distinguishing organizing documents from board policies and contracts, and identifying conflicts between the intended design and legally available mechanisms.

A useful legal package would connect each proposed mechanism to its upstream commitment, approving authority, practical implementation and residual limitations. A requirement does not disappear because a standard template omits it.

## What remains to be established

This overview makes no claim of incorporation, a selected legal jurisdiction, tax-exempt recognition, an appointed board or an engaged professional adviser. Those facts require their own evidence and recorded organizational acts.

It is not legal advice, a filing authorization or a claim of legal privilege. The founding Charter, governance framework and Code of Ethics remain candidate drafts until adopted through an explicit recorded process.

## Relationship to the doctrine

D01 explains the institutional rationale; D02 and D03 define the proposed commitments and their interpretation; D04 describes the governance mechanism; D06 and D16 address conduct and conformance. The legal work should preserve those outcomes without silently adopting or changing them.
