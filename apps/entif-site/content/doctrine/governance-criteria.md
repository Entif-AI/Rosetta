## 1. The obligation is the outcome

Entif should not demand precise, measurable, independently auditable governance from other institutions while offering only aspirations about its own behavior. A committee, an audit, a review, or a checklist may help produce a protected outcome. Their existence does not prove that the outcome occurred. This standard defines the evidence and authority needed to make the distinction visible. \[P09-P10, P14-P15, P23-P25; C09-C10, C14-C15, C23-C25; GC035.\]

The Governance worksheet of the Foundational Ethics and Doctrine Evidence Ledger v0.4.0 is the canonical machine-readable control register. This document is its human-readable standard. It preserves GC001-GC052 and explains how those controls are scoped, implemented, tested, enforced, remedied, and changed. It does not create a second independently maintained catalog. A change in a control's meaning must be reconciled in the Ledger and the affected documents through explicit change control.

All fifty-two controls are Candidate / Not Assessed in the source register for this pass. The requirements below are proposed requirements for adoption. They are not claims that Entif has adopted or implemented them. The implementation paths and test cases added here are first-draft proposals under the founder's instruction to fill missing details. The statements of external expectation reproduce Entif's normative criteria, not a claim that those criteria are current law everywhere.

D02 states constitutional commitments; D03 preserves meaning and provenance; D04 assigns institutional authority; D06 defines conduct duties. D07-D14 specialize this standard. #25 maps appropriate adopted controls to legal instruments. #26 records authority, version, adoption, exceptions, implementation state, and verification evidence. The actual organization may be small; its claims must still be exact.

## 2. Eight governing invariants

External/internal symmetry creates a rebuttable presumption of proportionate internal obligation when Entif publicly demands a governance property of others. An exception requires a material distinction, evidence, a responsible authority, and review. Smaller scale can justify simpler machinery. It cannot justify an undefined exemption from the protected outcome.

Substantive conformance requires evidence of the actual required outcome, or a clearly bounded and justified proxy. Process completion is evidence that a process ran. It is not automatically evidence that a right was protected, an error corrected, or a system made safe.

Power-scaled responsibility increases the burden of assurance as capability, reach, information advantage, dependency, preventability, control over recourse, speed, persistence, or irreversibility increases. Responsibility, causation, and culpability remain distinct. A party may owe restoration without malicious intent.

Authority separation prevents a materially conflicted actor from silently defining the rule, controlling the evidence, executing the decision, hearing its appeal, and certifying its own compliance. Separation must be real enough for the stakes, not merely represented by different agent names.

Evidence and receipts must permit reconstruction of material authority, observations, decisions, actions, exceptions, appeals, and remediation. The record must preserve uncertainty and source limitations. A receipt establishes what it actually witnesses, not universal truth.

Systemic correction addresses a recurring failure class rather than stopping at one reversal. Preserve proportionate evidence, identify causes and similarly affected cases where feasible, repair the mechanism, regression-test, and monitor recurrence.

Versioned change control prevents silent mutation of controls, metrics, interpretations, exceptions, and prior assurance. A changed model, tool, policy, data source, or deployment context can invalidate an earlier verification result.

Burden of justification rests with the authority seeking consequential deployment or expansion. Absence of visible harm or external complaints is not affirmative evidence that governance is sufficient. \[P23-P25; GC013, GC017, GC031-GC036, GC039, GC042-GC043, GC052.\]

## 3. Applicability and proportional machinery

Every material activity must have an applicability record identifying the actor or object, lifecycle stage, affected interests, deployment context, dependencies, and governing control slice. A control may be N/A only through a reasoned scope decision, not because it is inconvenient or unimplemented. A dormant control may remain applicable to future activation while requiring no current operational apparatus. Distinguish future applicability from present nonconformance.

Proposed assurance tiers are Routine, Material, High Consequence, and Critical. Routine covers bounded, reversible preparation with no material external effect or sensitive access beyond an existing authorization. Material covers actions affecting sensitive information, contractual commitments, access, funds, or a meaningful group of users. High Consequence covers substantial rights or welfare effects, extensive automation, weak exit options, irreversible state, privileged execution, or concentrated dependency. Critical covers plausible catastrophic or widespread severe harm, essential infrastructure dependence, uncontrolled propagation, or comparably severe and difficult-to-reverse consequences. The labels are draft local governance classes, not claims of equivalence to an external regulatory scheme.

Tier assignment must examine capability, autonomy, population and reach, duration, persistence, reversibility, information asymmetry, stakeholder dependence, substitution cost, control over recourse, externalized error cost, and the capability gap between operator and reviewer. A lower score on one dimension does not cancel a severe hazard on another. Record the highest material trigger and reasons for any disputed classification. D11 and D14 specialize this assessment for systems and experiments.

Routine work may use a short trace and self-check where there is no material conflict. Material work requires an assigned owner, explicit authorization, a targeted test, and a separated check when the same actor could otherwise conceal a consequential error. High-Consequence work requires a written assurance case, relevant independent expertise, tested containment, meaningful recourse, and reserved approval under D04. Critical work requires the strongest feasible independent review and explicit constitutional or other lawful authority appropriate to the decision; unresolved inability to demonstrate sufficient containment or protected outcomes blocks expansion. This standard does not manufacture an authority that the institution lacks.

Control intensity should scale through evidence, expertise, access, and actual protection, not through a larger number of forms. Any proposed exception must explain why the alternative machinery still protects the required outcome. A risk tier cannot be lowered simply because the review is expensive. \[GC008, GC025, GC035, GC042-GC043, GC047, GC052.\]

## 4. The control record and its owners

A material control record must answer: who or what is governed; scope and exclusions; trigger or threshold; required outcome; metric and measurement method; acceptable error or residual risk; decision authority; independent reviewer or separated counterparty; permitted and prohibited actions; notice and recourse; violation classification and consequence; case remedy and systemic follow-up; evidence requirements; duration and expiry; review cadence; amendment authority; adoption state; implementation state; and known gaps. Where a field does not apply, record why. A blank field must not be interpreted as permission.

The control owner implements the requirement and maintains evidence. The approving authority decides whether the proposed scope, tolerance, or exception is legitimate. The verifier tests the claim with the independence appropriate to the tier. The appeal authority reviews a contested decision without simply repeating the original owner's assertion. The registry custodian records those decisions but cannot create them. A role may combine administrative tasks at low risk, but conflicts and self-certification limits still apply.

D04's lawful board and delegation process assign actual holders. Until appointment, use the role and mark it Unassigned. The implementation assignments in the catalog below refer to proposed roles: Governance Lead, Research Lead, System Owner, Data Steward, Incident Lead, and Finance/Funding Lead. Their names do not establish that those posts exist. The independent chair or an appropriately unconflicted board authority oversees material governance decisions; qualified external review is required where internal separation is insufficient.

Each record links the current Ledger GC row, relevant P and C identifiers, governing documents, the source version, and evidence. The #26 registry records adoption and verified state; #37 tracks policy currentness; #36 consumes board reporting. Do not keep conflicting copies of a control's adoption state in unrelated operational spreadsheets.

## 5. Measurement and verification protocol

Before testing, specify the claim, governed population, unit of analysis, denominator, sampling period, baseline, measurement instrument, pass/fail threshold, error types, uncertainty, exclusions, and foreseeable ways the metric could be gamed. For a probabilistic system, document false positives and false negatives and who bears each cost. Do not select the threshold after seeing the result merely to obtain a pass. A revised threshold requires a new version and rationale.

There is no universal numerical error tolerance for every control. A missing required authorization or a knowingly false status report is a failed case; a statistical performance claim requires a predeclared, context-specific tolerance and uncertainty treatment. Where a tolerance is not yet determined, the control cannot claim that performance has been verified against an unstated standard. Record the missing decision and restrict the affected activity as its tier requires.

The verifier must inspect evidence sufficient to test the actual outcome, including relevant adverse cases and selection limits. Owner-selected demonstrations alone do not establish coverage. Use positive, negative, boundary, conflict, and recovery cases. Test revocation after delegation, appeal after a wrong decision, restoration after correction, and the effect of a material change on prior assurance. A process that rejects every request may appear safe on one metric while failing autonomy or false-positive requirements on another.

Record the executed method, inputs, version, environment, results, limitations, reviewer identity or role, conflicts, disposition, and evidence references. Separate planned tests from executed tests. Preserve failed tests and dissent. A second model can be a useful adversarial check; it must not be described as independent human or legal review. Verification expires or becomes stale when its assumptions no longer hold.

## 6. State transitions, exceptions, and consequences

Adoption states are Candidate, Adopted, Deferred, Rejected, and Superseded. Implementation states are Not Assessed, Planned, Partial, Implemented, Verified, Nonconformant, and N/A. These axes are independent. Adopted / Partial is possible and must remain visible. Implemented means the mechanism exists within its stated scope. Verified additionally requires the specified evidence and appropriate review. Neither state follows from the presence of a document.

A candidate becomes adopted only through a recorded act by competent authority identifying the version, scope, effective date, and transition. A rejected or superseded control retains its history and reason. #26 owns those records. A research finding may support a proposed change but cannot self-promote into authority by entering a registry.

Exceptions require a named owner, authority, scope, reason, affected interests, compensating measures, evidence, expiry, renewal criteria, and review. Proposed ordinary exceptions expire within thirty days unless a justified shorter interval applies. A longer period requires reserved approval and a stated basis. D04 governs shorter emergency expiry. No exception permits falsification, concealed retaliation, or silent Charter amendment. Repeated renewal triggers review of the underlying control design.

Violations receive a proportionate response under D04 and D06: correction; heightened supervision or restricted authority; suspension or removal; contract or other lawful remedies; disclosure; or competent external escalation. Assess severity, intent where supported, preventability, repetition, concealment, and authority. Restore affected interests and correct recurring mechanisms independently of the blame decision. A favorable aggregate metric cannot excuse a deliberate protected-floor violation.

## 7. The control catalog

The following entries preserve the Ledger's criterion, internal rule, scope, trigger, outcome, metric, and P/C/document crosswalk. Their implementation and test paragraphs are proposed operationalizations. Common authority, recourse, violation, remediation, evidence, expiry, and change-control semantics in sections 3-6 apply to every entry. Any more specific provision in the source register or a duly adopted specialized document also applies. The control owner's successful self-report is never the only evidence required where independent verification is triggered.

### GC001: Semantics & Scope / Criterion A

External expectation in the Ledger: Definitions and standards must be unambiguous enough that materially different interpretations cannot all claim compliance.

**Internal requirement:** Entif must define material governance terms, scopes, actors, states, and obligations precisely enough for independent interpretation and conformance testing.

**Scope:** All adopted governance, safety, research, product, and agent rules. Trigger: Before a rule governs material behavior or is cited as evidence of compliance. Required outcome: One controlled definition set with explicit scope, exclusions, ambiguity disposition, and canonical owner.

**Measurement basis:** Cross-review for conflicting interpretations; unresolved ambiguity register; conformance fixtures where applicable. Proposed implementation owner: Governance Lead. Maintain a controlled glossary with one owner per term and explicit boundary examples. Before adoption, two readers independently classify the same ambiguous cases. Conflicting classifications require a definition repair or an explicit unresolved interpretation; both cannot claim unqualified conformance. Test a rule with an undefined actor and a contradictory exception. It fails until the ambiguity has an accountable disposition.

### GC002: Measurement & Evidence / Criterion B

External expectation in the Ledger: Alignment rules require prescribed metrics and standardized measurement criteria capable of identifying misalignment, degradation, evasion, and regression.

**Internal requirement:** Entif must pair material controls with observable indicators, measurement methods, baselines, tolerances, and evidence sufficient to test the claimed outcome.

**Scope:** Material controls, evaluations, product safeguards, agent governance, research protocols. Trigger: When a rule makes a measurable claim or gates release/authority. Required outcome: Metrics and methods are specified before evaluation; process completion alone cannot substitute for outcome evidence.

**Measurement basis:** Metric definition, denominator, sampling, baseline, threshold, uncertainty, replication and receipt. Proposed implementation owner: Control Owner with separated verifier. Create a measurement specification before running the evaluation. Include denominator, sampling, baseline, uncertainty and both error directions. Test a favorable result produced by changing the denominator or threshold after inspection. The original claim fails; a revised evaluation must be separately versioned. Missing baseline or tolerance is a gap, not a passing score.

### GC003: Independence & Anti-Capture / Criterion C

External expectation in the Ledger: Governance should incorporate genuinely independent third-party ethical, safety, legal, human-rights, and public-interest expertise.

**Internal requirement:** Entif must require independent ethical or governance review when conflicts, consequence, novelty, or concentrated internal authority make self-review insufficient.

**Scope:** Board, research, high-impact product/agent decisions, sensitive experiments. Trigger: When defined conflict, risk, rights, or novelty thresholds are met. Required outcome: Reviewer independence, remit, evidence access, conflicts, dissent rights, and removal process are explicit.

**Measurement basis:** Conflict disclosures; reviewer selection provenance; access log; written disposition; dissent/minority report. Proposed implementation owner: Governance Lead. Use a conflict and competence declaration for each reviewer, grant access needed by the remit, and preserve dissent without management veto. Test an adverse finding involving the reviewer's funder. The review is not independent if that party can remove the finding or terminate the reviewer for reaching it; replace or supplement the review and record the limitation.

### GC004: Global Coordination / Criterion D

External expectation in the Ledger: Frontier-AI governance requires international accords, interoperable minimum standards, and coordinated enforcement rather than isolated national rulebooks.

**Internal requirement:** Entif should support legitimate interoperable global standards, map its controls to them, and avoid bespoke definitions used merely to claim easier compliance.

**Scope:** External standards participation; cross-border research/deployment; high-consequence systems. Trigger: When Entif operates across jurisdictions or contributes to standards with transnational consequences. Required outcome: Document applicable international obligations, equivalence mappings, gaps, and non-participant risk.

**Measurement basis:** Published standards crosswalk; jurisdiction matrix; unresolved incompatibilities; participation/ratification status where relevant. Proposed implementation owner: Governance Lead with qualified counsel. Maintain a scoped external-standard and jurisdiction crosswalk when transnational work actually begins. Record applicable versions, legal status, equivalences and conflicts. Test an activity that spans two incompatible requirements. The case cannot pass through silent selection of the weaker rule; obtain a recorded resolution and restrict the unresolved part of the activity.

### GC005: Enforcement & Remediation / Criterion E

External expectation in the Ledger: Rules require strict, specific, tiered consequences when obligations are bent, broken, concealed, or repeatedly ignored.

**Internal requirement:** Entif must define proportional consequence ladders for material governance breaches before relying on a control as enforceable.

**Scope:** Board, staff, contractors, agents, experiments, production systems. Trigger: When a material rule is violated, evaded, concealed, or repeatedly fails. Required outcome: Violation classes map to corrective action, authority restriction, independent review, suspension/removal, disclosure, or external escalation as appropriate.

**Measurement basis:** Violation classification; time-to-remediation; recurrence; consequence consistency; exception rate. Proposed implementation owner: Unconflicted board authority. Adopt violation classes and available lawful responses before relying on enforcement. Test the same material breach by an executive and a junior operator. Different outcomes require a reason based on consequence, authority or evidence, not status. Record notice, response opportunity, decision, appeal, remedy and verification; sanctions without a lawful or delegated basis do not pass.

### GC006: Global Coordination / Criterion F

External expectation in the Ledger: Collective capability, funding, and shared resources should exist for responding to rogue agentic or other transnational AI threats.

**Internal requirement:** Entif should define what capability or information it can contribute to legitimate shared emergency response and under what authority, privacy, security, and evidentiary limits.

**Scope:** Security, incident response, high-consequence agents, external coalitions. Trigger: When a credible incident exceeds Entif-only containment or has material cross-organizational effects. Required outcome: Predefined channels, authority, information-sharing limits, containment roles, handoffs, and post-incident review.

**Measurement basis:** Exercise results; response time; handoff completeness; evidence-sharing receipts; unresolved capacity gaps. Proposed implementation owner: Incident Lead. Identify legitimate escalation contacts, available contribution capacity and information-sharing limits. Run a tabletop in which an incident exceeds local containment. Verify that the handoff reaches an authorized recipient with sufficient evidence and no unnecessary private data. An untested contact list or assumed coalition membership is not response capacity; record the gap and corrective exercise.

### GC007: Semantics & Scope / Criterion G

External expectation in the Ledger: Regulation must specify which models, agents, deployments, capability levels, infrastructure providers, integrators, and downstream operators are actually governed.

**Internal requirement:** Entif must explicitly identify the regulated actor/object and ownership boundary for each material control.

**Scope:** Models, agents, tools, services, staff roles, vendors, integrations, research subjects. Trigger: Before a control is enforced or a release/authority decision is made. Required outcome: No material obligation may depend on an undefined actor, asset, lifecycle stage, jurisdiction, or control boundary.

**Measurement basis:** Coverage matrix; unowned-object count; ambiguous-owner exceptions. Proposed implementation owner: Control Owner. ### GC008: Semantics & Scope / Criterion H

External expectation in the Ledger: Capability and risk thresholds should trigger progressively stronger obligations before dangerous deployment conditions arise.

**Internal requirement:** Entif must define risk/capability tiers and the additional governance duties each tier activates.

**Scope:** Models, autonomous agents, experiments, external actions, critical integrations. Trigger: When capability, autonomy, scale, irreversibility, dependency, or consequence crosses defined thresholds. Required outcome: Higher consequence activates stronger evidence, approval, audit, containment, recourse, and review.

**Measurement basis:** Tier assignment reproducibility; threshold breach detection; override rate; post-hoc tier corrections. A tier reduction requires evidence and review; reducing the label to avoid a costly gate is a failed control.

### GC009: Independence & Anti-Capture / Criterion I

External expectation in the Ledger: Independent auditing must include meaningful technical access, conflict controls, reproducible methods, and authority to investigate beyond company-selected evidence.

**Internal requirement:** Entif audits must be scoped so reviewers can inspect the evidence necessary to test the claim rather than only artifacts chosen by the control owner.

**Scope:** Material governance, security, research, release, and compliance claims. Trigger: When independent verification is required by risk tier, conflict, adoption, or incident. Required outcome: Auditor independence, access rights, method, sampling, limitations, dissent, and reporting obligations are explicit.

**Measurement basis:** Evidence-access completeness; reproduction success; unresolved auditor findings; management overrides. Proposed implementation owner: Independent audit sponsor. Define the audit claim, evidence access, sampling rights and reporting limits before engagement. Test a request for an adverse case not selected by management. Denied material access must appear as a limitation or finding. An audit cannot certify the excluded claim; the owner must provide access or narrow the claim openly.

### GC010: Measurement & Evidence / Criterion J

External expectation in the Ledger: Consequential automated decisions require standardized provenance, logging, and evidence retention sufficient for reconstruction.

**Internal requirement:** Entif must preserve proportionate receipts tying material observations, evidence, authority, decisions, actions, overrides, appeals, and remediation together.

**Scope:** High-impact agent actions, policy interventions, research decisions, incidents. Trigger: When a decision materially affects rights, safety, data, authority, release, or irreversible state. Required outcome: Reconstruction must identify what happened, why, under which authority/version, with what evidence, and what changed afterward.

### GC011: Measurement & Evidence / Criterion K

External expectation in the Ledger: AI incidents need compulsory reporting with defined severity levels, deadlines, common schemas, protected channels, and penalties for concealment.

**Internal requirement:** Entif must define incident classes, reporting deadlines, evidence requirements, escalation paths, and protected reporting channels.

**Scope:** Operational incidents, research adverse events, governance failures, security/safety events. Trigger: When incident criteria or near-miss thresholds are met. ### GC012: Rights & Recourse / Criterion L

External expectation in the Ledger: People materially affected by automated decisions should receive meaningful notice, explanation, contestability, and appeal.

**Internal requirement:** Entif must not hide material decision-state changes from affected parties where notice is feasible and safe; consequential decisions need a usable correction path.

**Scope:** Users, research participants, partners, staff, affected third parties. Trigger: When an automated or delegated decision materially restricts access, rights, authority, data use, or opportunity. Required outcome: Notice identifies the state, basis, authority, practical next step, and appeal route proportionate to consequence.

**Measurement basis:** Notice coverage; explanation completeness; appeal access; reversal rate; time-to-resolution. Proposed implementation owner: Service Owner with independent recourse authority. The control fails if the party cannot discover the state or obtain an accountable challenge. Repair the display and notify affected people, subject to a specific reviewed safety limit.

### GC013: Independence & Anti-Capture / Criterion M

External expectation in the Ledger: Those who develop, deploy, evaluate, investigate, hear appeals, and certify remediation should not collapse into one unchecked authority.

**Internal requirement:** Entif must separate proposal, approval, execution, review, appeal, and audit powers when consequence or conflict warrants it.

**Scope:** Board powers, agent authority, releases, incidents, appeals, policy changes. Trigger: When a single actor could otherwise control a material decision end-to-end. ### GC014: Semantics & Scope / Criterion N

External expectation in the Ledger: Rules must specify how safety, autonomy, rights, privacy, competition, commercial interests, national security, and public welfare are prioritized when they conflict.

**Internal requirement:** Entif must make material value-conflict rules explicit rather than allowing hidden priority ordering to emerge from incentives or implementation defaults.

**Scope:** High-impact governance, safety, product, research, and agent decisions. Trigger: When protected values materially conflict. Required outcome: Priority rules, tie-breakers, least-invasive alternatives, dissent, and escalation are explicit and reviewable.

**Measurement basis:** Conflict-decision receipts; override reasons; least-invasive-alternative usage; unresolved conflicts. Proposed implementation owner: Approving authority under D04. ## 8. Specialized documents and legal embodiment

Each specialized document must name its applicable GC slice and state any stricter domain rule with its parent control. The Charter constitutionalizes durable principles without importing the entire catalog. D03 preserves interpretation and dissent. D04 supplies the institutional machinery. D06 turns controls into participant duties. #25 must classify adopted controls by legal home: organizing instrument, bylaws, resolution, policy, contract, delegation, or operational standard. A legal conflict requires a reasoned disposition, not a silent weakening. #26 records adoption and conformance separately. The originating Ledger row remains the semantic reference; support registers may link to it but must not independently change its meaning.

The organization should eventually classify each relevant Skill or workflow as having no governance dependency, read-only resolution, a mandatory control crosswalk, candidate-delta production, or authorized adoption/change authority. That integration is a later implementation pass. This draft does not claim that existing Skills have been audited or updated. A Skill cannot acquire adoption authority merely by reading this document.

## 9. Review and initial adoption packet

Proposed review is quarterly for material control gaps and exceptions, annually for the full standard, and immediately after a serious governance failure or a change that invalidates assurance. External mappings require review when their source changes. The board packet should show adopted-but-unimplemented controls, stale verification, overdue remedies, expired exceptions, reviewer capacity gaps, and material dissent. Do not aggregate these into a green status that hides an unresolved critical condition.

The adoption packet must identify the exact D16 version and Ledger snapshot, controlling Charter and governance versions, applicable current activities, assigned owners, proposed tolerances and review cadence, unimplemented requirements, transitional restrictions, and the lawful adopting authority. A staged adoption may define a narrower initial operational scope while preserving the escalation rule for future capability. It must not label every unbuilt safeguard N/A to create the appearance of full compliance.

A completed first draft means the control semantics and proposed implementation paths are available for review. Operational adoption, assignment of actual role holders, executed tests, independent verification, and proof of effective remedies remain separate work. The initial register retains all fifty-two controls at Candidate / Not Assessed unless a later recorded act and evidence justify a transition.

## 10. First-pass open decisions and validation

The assurance-tier boundaries, proposed exception limit, review cadence, reviewer assignments, system-specific error tolerances, reporting channels, and resources require adoption or operational evidence. They are not missing because the control has no meaning: the standard specifies who must decide, what evidence is needed, and what cannot be claimed before that decision. Specialized documents may supply a stricter rule with explicit cross-reference.
